Draw a little kit, line it up, export one skin

A skin is built from just eight pieces of art: a standalone little cat, a head/middle/tail set that composes into a "worm" of any straight length, and four whole "fat" characters for solid 2×2 / 3×3 / 3×2 / 2×3 blocks. Every game piece — even bent ones like L, T, S and Z shapes — is automatically split into straight runs and lone singles, and a fat block only stays one big character while it's fully intact. The moment a line-clear removes even one of its cells, it's redrawn on the spot as fresh runs — so nothing ever shows up as a broken half-body. Scroll down to see this live.

The eight parts

Head, middle and tail rotate automatically to build a run of any length, in either direction. The four fat characters only need to look good as one whole picture — they don't need to connect to anything.

How to line things up well

Turn on "Show guide" on a part to see the 96px-wide connector band the game expects at each edge. Keep the same band width, position and color on every part that touches another — head's right edge, middle's left and right edges, and tail's left edge should all match — so a run of any length reads as one continuous body with no visible seams. Drag the artwork to slide it, and use the slider to grow or shrink it inside the frame.

The two live preview sections below assemble real runs and real game shapes from your current art — including a working "simulate a line clear" demo on the fat blocks — so you can catch a bad seam or a broken-looking transformation before you export.

Prefer drawing in Procreate or another app first? Use "Guide PNG" on any part for a reference image sized exactly right, with the connector band marked in green.
