Draw a little kit, line it up, export one skin

Nothing here is built by gluing pieces together anymore. A skin is nine fully hand-drawn images: one complete picture for each straight run length from 1 to 5 cells long (the longest any piece can ever produce), plus four whole "fat" characters for solid 2×2 / 3×3 / 3×2 / 2×3 blocks. Bent pieces — L, T, S, Z, corners — don't get their own art at all: they're automatically split into whichever runs and singles best describe their shape (a T piece, for example, is just your 3-long picture plus your 1-long picture), so you only ever have to draw the straight lengths and the fat blocks. The nine parts

Each run length is one complete drawing, authored lying flat and facing right — the game rotates it 90° for a vertical placement (the front ends up on top). The four fat characters just need to look good as one whole picture on their own.

Tips for a consistent-looking set

There's no seam to match anymore — each length is its own finished picture — but it still helps to keep the character's proportions (head size, body height, line weight, palette) consistent across run-1 through run-5, so the same little cat just reads as "longer," not like a different character. Turn on "Show guide" for light cell-boundary lines while you draw.
